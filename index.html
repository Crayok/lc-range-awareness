These diagrams and scenarios are for the people who hold the range roles. Their purpose is to find errors in the procedures before the procedures are used on a range.

Simple view

Every event on the range changes one item on the status display, and each site's state is worked out from those items.

The display never decides anything. It shows what was reported, people act on it, and what they do becomes the next event.

Detailed view

For each site the display tracks its colour, the readiness items that feed it, and its state. Each one changes only on a named trigger from a named position. Every arrow below is a claim about the procedure that a role holder can challenge.

Site colour is the only item shown in colour, because it is site status from a danger point of view. Readiness and site state are words. SAFING is the word used here for the wind-down: tanks off, pads safed. It is the common launch-operations term for making a vehicle and pad safe. "Securing" is the nearest alternative if you prefer it.

Questions this diagram already raises, for the people in the roles to settle:

  • Two sites at once. Can two sites be in their final phase together, or does the RCO run one count at a time?

  • A critical alert at one site. Does it stop every site that has an event under way? The board assumes it does.

  • Recovery at HOLD. Restricted and Statics may go RED with Road at HOLD. Does the same apply to Recovery?

  • What ends SAFING. What must be true at each site before it reads OPEN again?

  • An unattended armed site. If a storm clears a site with electronics armed, what colour does the display show?

  • Who may move another position's item. Can the RCO set Road to HOLD if Road Control cannot be reached?

Three earlier questions were settled on 2 October 2026. A site reads OPEN until an event starts, so HOLD is used only during an event. A team at HOLD is the site LCO's decision: wait on the team's estimate, or move on to the next rocket. Advanced cannot go RED while Road is at HOLD, and Restricted and Statics can.

This revision also carries the decisions of 4 and 7 October 2026. There is a Chief RSO, with an RSO and an LCO at each site. The site RSO approves YELLOW and RED and the RCO is informed. The site RSO is polled, and the RCO and the Chief RSO are not: each is GO until they set HOLD or NO-GO, for one site or several. Pad numbers are assigned per salvo by the site LCO. Site clear is a count of people in against people out. Road closures are per salvo and per site, held by Road Monitors. Each site defines its own muster points.

Tabletop scenarios

Nineteen scenarios, three or four for each site area. Each is written to put pressure on one part of the procedure, and each ends with questions the procedures do not yet answer.

How to run one

  1. Seat one person in each position: RCO, Chief RSO, the LCO and the RSO for the scenario's site, Net Control, Road Control, Recovery Control, a Pad Manager and at least one team lead. One more person is the facilitator.

  2. Set the status board to the scenario's starting state and read the setup aloud.

  3. Read one inject at a time. People answer as they would on the loop, using the standard calls, and change their own items on the board.

  4. After each inject, compare what happened with the expected response. Note every difference, including where the group thinks the expected response is wrong.

  5. Finish with the probe questions. Record each answer as a proposed change to the procedures.

The expected responses come from the draft procedures. Where the draft is silent, the expected response says so.

Restricted site

P1: The count does not match

Salvo 2 at the Restricted site. Igniters are going in and the install crews are walking back.

Start: Restricted RED, phase ARM, 6 in and 4 out, Road GO, LCO HOLD, RSO HOLD. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 4 GO, Pad 2 Team 9 GO, Pad 3 Team 12 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. Pad Manager: "Restricted LCO, Pad Restricted, pad clear, six in, five out."The LCO does not accept pad clear. The board reads six in, five out, the site cannot go past HOLD, and the reason is logged.
2. A team lead says the sixth person walked back early with a toolbox and is in the assembly tent.Someone confirms the person by sight and the check-in record is corrected. A verbal assurance alone does not clear the count.
3. The RCO asks whether the poll can start now to save time.The poll waits. The site cannot go past HOLD until the count matches, and then the RCO opens the poll.

Probe: Who physically confirms where the sixth person is, and how is that reported? Where is the check-in record, and who is allowed to correct it?

P2: Misfire, then smoke

Salvo 2 at the Restricted site. The first flight is away and Team 9 on pad 2 is next.

Start: Restricted RED, phase LAUNCH, 6 in and 6 out, Road GO, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 4 flown, Pad 2 Team 9 GO, Pad 3 Team 12 GO, Pad 4 Team 15 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. Five count for pad 2. The LCO fires. Nothing happens.The LCO checks the right pad was selected and chooses press, recycle or hold. The event is logged.
2. Thirty seconds later a thin line of smoke rises from pad 2.HOLD. The site stays closed and the Restricted RSO takes direction. An alert names pad 2. The response team is readied but does not move until the Chief RSO says so.
3. Team 9's lead asks to go out and pull the igniter.Refused. Nobody approaches during the salvo, and afterwards only with the Restricted RSO's approval.
4. The smoke stops after two minutes. Teams 12 and 15 ask to carry on.The Restricted RSO decides. If the hold has passed five minutes, the poll is run again before any count.

Probe: How long is the wait before anyone approaches a misfired rocket? The procedure has no number. Who decides whether pads 3 and 4 can fly next to a suspect rocket on pad 2?

P3: Telemetry drops before the count

Salvo 2 at the Restricted site. Team 4 has flown and Team 9 is next.

Start: Restricted RED, phase LAUNCH, 6 in and 6 out, Road GO, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 4 flown, Pad 2 Team 9 GO, Pad 3 Team 12 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. The LCO asks Team 9 for its GO. The team lead answers: "HOLD, we have lost telemetry."Team 9 goes to HOLD on the display. The LCO does not count Team 9. The site is not held, because Team 12 is GO.
2. The LCO asks how long. The team says two or three minutes.The LCO decides on that estimate: wait for Team 9, or move on to Team 12.
3. The LCO moves on and Team 12 flies. Team 9 still has no telemetry.Team 9 stays at HOLD on its pad. It can fly later in the salvo if it reports GO, or it declares NO-GO and waits for close-out.
4. Team 9 asks to send one person to the pad to power-cycle the rocket.Refused while the site is RED. The rocket is made safe at close-out.

Probe: How long an estimate is short enough to wait for? When a skipped team reports GO again, does anyone re-check it before the count?

Advanced site

A1: The fill time arrives and the road is not clear

Advanced salvo. The pad is clear, the team is GO and the agreed fill time is five minutes away.

Start: Advanced YELLOW, phase ARM, 4 in and 4 out, Road HOLD, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 7 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. Road Control: the sweep found a pickup parked inside the closure. The owner is being found.Advanced Road stays at HOLD with the reason noted. The poll cannot complete.
2. The fill time arrives. The team lead asks the LCO to request RED.Refused. The Advanced RSO does not approve RED while Road is at HOLD, because the fill is time sensitive. The RCO sets a new fill time with the team.
3. The team says it would rather not wait more than twenty minutes.The RCO weighs the team's limit. Nothing is filled to save time.
4. Twelve minutes late, Road Control reports GO.The RCO completes the poll. At the new fill time the LCO requests RED, the Advanced RSO approves, and the RCO is informed.

Probe: Who tells Recovery, Road and the PA audience that the fill time has moved? What happens if the new fill time is later than the team can accept?

A2: Link lost during fill

Advanced salvo. Fill started six minutes ago and nobody is at the pad.

Start: Advanced RED, phase LAUNCH, 4 in and 4 out, Road GO, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 7 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. Team lead: "We have lost the link to our fill controller."HOLD, then abort. The team goes to NO-GO and a critical alert is raised. The detank clock starts.
2. The team believes the vehicle is venting by itself but cannot confirm it.The pad stays RED. Nobody approaches on belief alone.
3. A team member offers to walk out and read the tank gauge.Refused until the Advanced RSO approves an approach.
4. Twenty minutes later the link returns and the tank reads zero.The team calls "depressurized". The Advanced RSO may now approve an approach and the colour steps back.

Probe: Without telemetry, what counts as proof that the vehicle is depressurized? Who approaches first, from where, and with what protection?

A3: An aircraft while the vehicle is full

Advanced salvo. Fill completed two minutes ago. The team stated a ten-minute hold limit.

Start: Advanced RED, phase LAUNCH, 4 in and 4 out, Road GO, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 7 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. The Chief RSO sees a light aircraft heading toward the restricted airspace.The Chief RSO sets Airspace to HOLD, and every site that needs airspace holds. The team puts the vehicle in its hold state.
2. Five minutes on, the aircraft is still circling.Someone is watching the hold clock and calls the time remaining.
3. At nine minutes the aircraft leaves. The team says it is still GO.The hold passed five minutes, so the procedure calls for the poll again before any count. The group decides whether that fits inside the limit.
4. The LCO asks for clear to count.Clear to count only with every polled position GO and no HOLD from the RCO or the Chief RSO. Otherwise the team aborts and vents.

Probe: Who owns the hold clock, the team or the range? Does the five-minute re-poll rule work for a filled vehicle with a ten-minute limit?

Statics

S1: Static test with the road on hold

A team is ready for a hot fire at the static test site. The stand is clear and the agreed fill time has arrived.

Start: Statics YELLOW, phase ARM, 4 in and 4 out, Road HOLD, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Test Cell 1 Team 5 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. The Statics LCO requests RED to begin filling. Road is at HOLD while a Road Monitor is relieved.Allowed, if the Statics RSO approves. Statics follows the restricted rule and may go RED with Road at HOLD. The test GO still needs Road at GO.
2. Fill is complete. Road is still at HOLD.The site holds. The team states how long it can hold when full.
3. The restricted site reports pad clear and its LCO asks for the poll.The RCO decides which site goes first. The procedures do not say whether two sites can be in a final count together.
4. Road reports GO for Statics.The RCO polls for the static test first, then for the restricted salvo.

Probe: May a static test and a launch be in their final count at the same time? Who sets the order between sites, the RCO or the site LCOs?

S2: The engine does not light

Static test site. The vehicle is filled, the stand is clear and the RCO has given clear to count.

Start: Statics RED, phase TEST, 4 in and 4 out, Road GO, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Test Cell 1 Team 5 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. Five count. The team commands ignition. Nothing lights and the tank is still full.HOLD. The team reports what its telemetry shows. Nobody approaches the stand.
2. The team wants to try again at once.The Statics LCO decides with the Statics RSO: one more attempt inside the fill-to-fire clock, or abort.
3. The second attempt also fails. The team's hold limit has been reached.Abort. The team vents and detanks, and the site moves to SAFING.
4. The team reports the tank at zero and asks to approach the stand.The Statics RSO approves the approach. The colour steps back to GREEN and the site returns to OPEN.

Probe: How many ignition attempts are allowed on one fill? What must be true at the static site before SAFING ends and the site reads OPEN?

S3: Fire after a static test

Static test site. The burn ended normally thirty seconds ago and the site is winding down. A restricted salvo is arming at the same time.

Start: Restricted YELLOW, phase ARM, 6 in and 0 out, Road GO, LCO HOLD, RSO HOLD. Statics RED, phase POST, 4 in and 4 out, Road GO, LCO HOLD, RSO HOLD. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Statics Test Cell 1 Team 5 fired, Restricted Pad 1 Team 4 HOLD, Restricted Pad 2 Team 9 HOLD.

InjectExpected response
1. Grass near the test stand is burning.A critical alert is raised. The Chief RSO directs the fire team. Statics stays RED.
2. The team's telemetry shows oxidizer still in the tank.The fire team does not approach the stand until the team has vented and the Statics RSO says it is safe to approach.
3. Restricted teams are at their pads arming electronics.The RCO and the Chief RSO decide whether the Restricted site stops work. Either can set HOLD or NO-GO on that one site. On the board a critical alert puts every site that is in an event at NO-GO.
4. The fire is out and the tank reads zero.The Chief RSO declares the area safe and the alert is resolved. SAFING completes and Statics returns to GREEN and OPEN.

Probe: Does a critical alert at one site stop the others? Where does the fire team stage during a static test?

Road and spectator

R1: A vehicle goes around the barrier

Salvo 2 at the Restricted site. Team 4 has flown and the LCO is about to count Team 9.

Start: Restricted RED, phase LAUNCH, 6 in and 6 out, Road GO, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 4 flown, Pad 2 Team 9 GO, Pad 3 Team 12 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. A Road Monitor at the north closure radios Road Control: an ATV has gone around the barrier toward the launch area.Road Control calls "HOLD, HOLD, HOLD" on the range loop and sets Road to HOLD.
2. The LCO is at "three" in the five count.The LCO stops the count and safes the controller. The time from the Road Monitor's call to the stop is noted.
3. The ATV stops 200 m inside. The driver works for the landowner.Launches stay suspended. The area is cleared and swept again.
4. The Road Monitor escorts the ATV out. Six minutes have passed.Road reports GO. The hold passed five minutes, so the poll is run again.

Probe: How many relays are there between the Road Monitor and the LCO, and how long do they take? May a Road Monitor call HOLD directly on the range loop?

R2: Road Monitors not in position

Salvo 3 is loaded at the Restricted site. YELLOW has just been approved and Road Control is standing up the closure.

Start: Restricted YELLOW, phase ARM, 8 in and 4 out, Road HOLD, LCO HOLD, RSO HOLD. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 4 HOLD, Pad 2 Team 9 HOLD, Pad 3 Team 12 HOLD.

InjectExpected response
1. Road Control: two of four Road Monitors are still at lunch. Ten minutes.Road stays at HOLD with the reason noted.
2. The LCO asks whether igniters can go in before the Road Monitors are in place, since the closure only matters at launch.Allowed if the Restricted RSO approves RED. The Restricted site may go RED while its Road is at HOLD. The poll cannot complete until Road is GO.
3. The Launch Director points out that the day is an hour behind.The schedule is not a reason to change a status. Each status stays with its owner.
4. The Road Monitors are in place and the sweep is done.Road reports GO.

Probe: How long may rockets sit armed while Road catches up? Does the same allowance apply when Recovery is at HOLD?

R3: Medical emergency in the spectator area

Salvo 2 at the Restricted site. Igniters are in, the pad is clear and the poll is about to start.

Start: Restricted RED, phase ARM, 6 in and 6 out, Road GO, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 4 GO, Pad 2 Team 9 GO, Pad 3 Team 12 GO.

InjectExpected response
1. A Road Monitor: "BREAK, BREAK, BREAK. A spectator has collapsed at the viewing line."All other traffic stops. The RCO sets NO-GO at every site and each LCO confirms the controller is safed.
2. The medic asks for an ambulance. The access road runs through the closed section.The Chief RSO directs the response. Net Control calls by satellite phone when the Chief RSO or Launch Director says so.
3. Three rockets are on the pads with igniters in.The Chief RSO decides, with the Restricted RSO, whether the pads must be made safe before the ambulance uses the road.
4. The ambulance leaves forty minutes later.The accident contingency plan is completed. Restart needs the Launch Director's agreement and a full poll.

Probe: Does the ambulance route pass inside the safety distance of a RED pad? Who meets the ambulance and guides it in?

Recovery

V1: A recovery team misses a check-in

Salvo 3 is arming at the Restricted site. Two recovery teams from salvo 2 are still out.

Start: Restricted YELLOW, phase ARM, 6 in and 0 out, Road GO, LCO HOLD, RSO HOLD. Recovery at HOLD, other range items GO. Pad 1 Team 3 HOLD, Pad 2 Team 8 HOLD, Pad 3 Team 11 HOLD.

InjectExpected response
1. Recovery team B misses its check-in.Recovery Control calls the team and keeps Recovery at HOLD.
2. Two more calls get no answer. The last position was 1.2 km north-west.Recovery Control tells the Chief RSO. The group decides whether this is HOLD or NO-GO.
3. The LCO requests RED for salvo 3.The Restricted RSO does not approve RED while a team is unaccounted for.

V2: Ballistic return toward a recovery team

Salvo 2 at the Restricted site. Recovery team A is deployed 800 m north-west, at what was judged a safe location.

Start: Restricted RED, phase LAUNCH, 6 in and 6 out, Road GO, LCO GO, RSO GO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 4 flown, Pad 2 Team 9 flown, Pad 3 Team 12 GO, Pad 4 Team 15 GO.

InjectExpected response

V3: A bear on the recovery trail

Salvo 2 is closed out and recovery teams are being released.

Start: Restricted GREEN, phase POST, 6 in and 6 out, Road HOLD, LCO HOLD, RSO HOLD. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Pad 1 Team 4 flown, Pad 2 Team 9 flown, Pad 3 Team 12 flown.

InjectExpected response

C4: Lightning with rockets armed

Salvo 3 is arming at the Restricted site. A rocket has been raised on the advanced pad.

Start: Restricted YELLOW, phase ARM, 6 in and 0 out, Road GO, LCO HOLD, RSO HOLD. Advanced GREEN, phase PREP, 4 in and 0 out, Road HOLD, LCO HOLD, RSO HOLD. Recovery, Weather and Airspace GO. Restricted Pad 1 Team 3 HOLD, Restricted Pad 2 Team 8 HOLD, Advanced Pad 1 Team 7 HOLD.

InjectExpected response
1. The lightning detector shows a strike at 19 km.Weather goes to HOLD. Work is brought to a stopping point.
2. Five minutes later there is a strike at 14 km.Weather goes to NO-GO and the hazardous areas are evacuated, whatever state the work is in.

Tabletop tool

The easiest platform to code to your sites and statuses is a plain web page, and a first version is built: the Range Status Board. It follows the minimum viable awareness system in the framework: manual status input, an automatic range state, a phase display, alerts and an event log.

What the board does:

  • Site colour for Restricted, Advanced and Statics, with people in and people out for each. While the two differ, the site cannot go past HOLD.

  • Site state for each site: OPEN until an event starts, then GO, HOLD or NO-GO, then SAFING until the site is reopened.

  • Pads. The site LCO assigns each team a pad number, with its team name and team number. Each salvo starts at Pad 1, Statics uses Test Cell 1, and the numbers clear when the site reopens for the next salvo.

  • Readiness on each site card, in poll order: the teams, Road, the site LCO and the site RSO. Recovery, Weather and Airspace are range-wide. Statics needs Road and Weather only.

  • RCO and Chief RSO. Not polled. Each is GO at every site until set to HOLD or NO-GO, for one site from its card or for every site at once.

  • The two site rules. Advanced is refused RED while its Road is not GO. A team at HOLD does not hold the site, and the board prompts the LCO to wait or move on.

  • Alerts and an event log. Every change is logged with the time and the position that made it, and it notes when someone changes an item they do not own.

  • A cross-check that lists items which disagree, such as an LCO reporting GO while the people count does not match, or two sites in their final phase together.

  • All nineteen scenarios. Picking one sets the starting state. The facilitator reveals injects one at a time and can show the expected response.
